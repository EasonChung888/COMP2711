 Example: There is a test for a particular disease.
     The test’s false negative rate is 1%, i.e., it gives a
      negative result with prob. 1% when given to
      someone with the disease.
     The test’s false positive rate is 0.5%, i.e., it gives a
      positive result with prob. 0.5% when given to
      someone without the disease.
     On average, one person out of 100,000 has the
      disease.
 Question:
  Should someone who tests positive be worried?
