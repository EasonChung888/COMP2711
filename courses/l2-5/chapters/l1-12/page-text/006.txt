 Euclid’s Division Theorem

                                             Euclid
 Theorem:                             (325 B.C.E.–265B.C.E.)
  For any 𝑎   ∈  𝐙,𝑑 ∈ 𝐙+, there exist unique      integers 𝑞   and
  𝑟, with 0  ≤  𝑟 < 𝑑, such that 𝑎    = 𝑑𝑞  + 𝑟.
     𝑑 is called the  divisor.
     𝑎 is called the dividend.         Notation:
     𝑞 is called the  quotient.
     𝑟 is called the remainder.              𝑞 = 𝑎 div 𝑑
 Examples                                    𝑟 = 𝑎 mod   𝑑
     11 div 3 = 3,  11  mod  3 =  2.
     −11  div 3 = −4,   −11  mod  3  = 1.
