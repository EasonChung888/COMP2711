 General counterexample to show that we cannot
 distribute an existential quantifier over a conjunction

▪ ∃x (P(x) ^   Q(x))  is not equivalent to      ∃x P(x) ^ ∃x Q(x).
▪ The domain contains only two elements a and b such
  that P(a) is T, Q(a) is F, P(b) is F, Q(b) is T.
▪ What is the truth value of        ∃x (P(x) ^   Q(x))?
▪ False because both        P(a) ^  Q(a) and P(b) ^     Q(b) are F.
▪ What is the truth value of        ∃x P(x)?
▪ True because P(a) is T
▪ What is the truth value of        ∃x Q(x)?
▪ True because Q(b) is T
▪ What is the truth value of        ∃x P(x) ^ ∃x Q(x)?
▪ True.
