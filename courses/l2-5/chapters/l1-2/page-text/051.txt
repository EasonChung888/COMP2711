 Example 1: Is ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) → ∃y Q(y) equivalent to
 ∃𝑥∃y (𝑃(𝑥) →  Q(y)) (already PNF)?
▪  ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) → ∃y Q(y) ≡ ¬ ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) ∨ ∃y Q(y)
Consider the domains:
Both ¬ ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) and ∃y Q(y)  are false      .
Therefore, ∃𝑥𝑃(𝑥)→∃y Q(y)≡¬∃𝑥𝑃(𝑥)∨∃y Q(y)≡F
 ▪ ∃𝑥∃y (𝑃(𝑥) →  Q(y)) ≡ ∃𝑥∃y (¬ 𝑃(𝑥) ∨ Q(y))
Using the above domains we have True for
pairs (𝑃(b),Q(a)), (𝑃(b),Q(b)) because ¬ 𝑃(b) is T.
▪  Explanation of non equivalence using PNF of ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) → ∃y Q(y):
∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) → ∃y Q(y) ≡ ¬ ∃𝑥 𝑃(𝑥) ∨ ∃y Q(y) ≡
∀𝑥 ¬𝑃(𝑥) ∨ ∃y Q(y) ≡ by rule 2 ∀𝑥 (¬𝑃(𝑥) ∨ ∃y Q(y)) ≡ by rule 4
∀𝑥∃y (¬𝑃(𝑥) ∨  Q(y)) ≡ ∀𝑥∃y (𝑃(𝑥) →  Q(y))
▪  ∀𝑥∃y (𝑃(𝑥) →  Q(y)) not equivalent to ∃𝑥∃y (𝑃(𝑥) →  Q(y))
▪  Although the expressions seem similar in their original form, they
   are different in PNF
