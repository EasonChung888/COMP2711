   Example on Restricted Domains 1
▪  Let P(x), Q(x), and R(x) be the statements “x is a lion”, “x is fierce”,
   and “x drinks coffee”, respectively.
   Assume that the domain consists of all creatures
▪  Consider the following argument:
1.  Premise 1: “All lions are fierce”
2.  Premise 2: “Some lions do not drink coffee”
3.  Conclusion: “Some fierce creatures do not drink coffee”
▪  Express the statements using quantifiers and P(x), Q(x), and R(x).
1.  ∀x (P(x) →   Q(x))
2.  ∃x (P(x) ^  ¬ R(x))
3.  ∃x (Q(x) ^   ¬ R(x))

▪  Remark: In the next classes, we will discuss the issue of
   determining whether the conclusion is a valid consequence of the
   premises. In this example, it is.
