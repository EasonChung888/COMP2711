  Modus Ponens: The Most Basic Rule

▪  If p is true and p → q is true, then q must be also true.
▪  Premises: p, p → q
▪  Conclusion: q
▪  Tautology: [(p ^ (p → q)] →      q
▪  Proof that [(p ^ (p → q)] →     q ≡ True (independently of p and q)
[(p ^ (p → q)] →   q ≡ implication equivalence
¬[(p ^ (¬p ∨ q)] ∨  q ≡ De Morgan
[¬p ∨ ¬(¬p ∨ q)] ∨   q ≡ De Morgan
[¬p ∨ (p ^ ¬q)] ∨   q ≡ Distibutive
[(¬p ∨ p) ^ (¬p ∨ ¬q)] ∨   q ≡ Negation Law (¬p ∨ p) is T
[(T ^ (¬p ∨ ¬q)] ∨  q ≡ Identity Law (T ^ (¬p ∨ ¬q)) is (¬p ∨ ¬q)
(¬p ∨ ¬q) ∨  q ≡ Associative
¬p ∨ (¬q ∨   q) ≡ Negation Law (¬q ∨ q) is T
