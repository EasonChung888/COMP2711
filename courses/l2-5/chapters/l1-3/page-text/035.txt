 Proof by Contradiction

▪ Suppose we want to prove that a statement p is true.

▪ Instead, we assume       p is false, i.e., ¬ p is true. Then,
  using axioms, definitions, previously proved
  theorems, and rules of inference, we derive a
  contradiction F. This means that our assumption that
  “¬p is true” is false.  Consequently,  p must be true.

▪ If you want to prove p, you show ¬p →            F
▪ An   example     of  contradiction     is r  ^  ¬r,  where   r  is a
  proposition, .i.e., ¬p → r ^ ¬r.
