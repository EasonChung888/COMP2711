Exercise: Knight and Knave Puzzle 1

There are two kinds of inhabitants on an island, knights, who always tell
the truth, and knaves, who always lie. You encounter two people A and B.
▪  A says: “B is a knight” (q)
▪  B says: “A is a knight” (p)
Question: What you can say about A and B (knight/knave)?
Lets define the propositions p :“A is a knight” and q:“B is a knight”. Knights
tell the truth, which means that their propositions are true. Knaves tell lies,
which means that their propositions are false.
1] Assume that A is a knight (p is true). Then, according to his statement, q
is true. Since B is a knight, his statement p is true, which is consistent with
the assumption. Therefore, both A and B are knights (p              ∧ q).
2] Assume that A is a knave (p is false). Then, according to his statement, q
is false. Since B is a knave, his statement p is false, which is consistent with
the assumption. Therefore, both A and B are knaves (¬p               ∧ ¬ q).
Thus, I can conclude that A and B are of the same type ((p             ∧ q) ∨(¬p   ∧ ¬ q)).
