   Exercise: Differences between human and
   propositional logic

A real experiment was performed many years ago to study the relationship
between human and propositional logic.
Human subjects were given the rule: “If the envelope is sealed, it must
contain a stamp on the other side”, and were asked to turn the minimum
number of envelopes in order to verify the rule.
An alternative description: you work in the post-office and you want to make
sure that all sealed envelopes have stamps, by turning the minimum
number of envelopes. There are 4 cases:
1: You are shown a sealed envelope – do you need to turn it to check
whether it contains a stamp?
Yes. I turn it over to make sure that it contains a stamp.
In terms of propositional logic you can think of
a] p as the proposition “the envelope is sealed”,
b] q as the proposition “the envelope contains a stamp”.
c] The rule is p→q
Since p is true, you must check that q is also true. If ¬q, p→q is F.
