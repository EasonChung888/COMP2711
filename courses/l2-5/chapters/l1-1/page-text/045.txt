Knights and Knaves Revisited

There are two kinds of inhabitants on an island, knights, who always tell
the truth, and knaves, who always lie.
Let propositions p be “A is knight” and 𝑞 be “B is knight”
You encounter two people A and B.
▪  A says: “We are both knaves”: ¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞
▪  B says nothing
Question: What you can say about A and B (knight/knave)? Two cases:
1. If A's is a knight then both 𝑝, and ¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞 must be true. However, 𝑝 and
   ¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞 cannot both be true at the same time (contradiction). Case 1 is
   not possible.
▪  If A's is a knave, then ¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞 is false, i.e.,¬ (¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞) is true. Using De
   Morgan's law,¬ (¬𝑝 ∧ ¬𝑞) ≡ 𝑝 ∨ 𝑞. Since A is a knave,           𝑝 is false. Therefore,
   𝑞 must be true. Conclusion: A is knave, B is knight.
