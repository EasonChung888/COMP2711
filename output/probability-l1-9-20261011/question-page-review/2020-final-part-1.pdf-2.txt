Now consider n = k + 1: As 2 k | Ak, there are two possibilities for the
remainder of Ak dividing 2 k+1. Ak = 2 k · l, where l is even, which means
Ak ≡ 0 (mod 2 k+1), or Ak = 2 k · (1 + l′), where l′ is even, which means
Ak ≡ 2k (mod 2 k+1)
1. Ak ≡ 0 (mod 2 k+1). In this scenario we add the digit 2 to the left side
of Ak. So Ak+1 = 2Ak. We show that 2 k+1 | Ak+1.
Ak+1 = 2 · 10k + Ak = 2 · 2k · 5k + Ak = 2k+1 · 5k + Ak. And we know
that 2 k+1 · 5k ≡ 0 (mod 2 k+1) and Ak ≡ 0 (mod 2 k+1). So Ak+1 ≡ 0
(mod 2 k+1).
2. Ak ≡ 2k (mod 2 k+1). In this scenario we add the digit 1 to the left
side of Ak. So Ak+1 = 1Ak. We show that 2 k+1 | Ak+1.
Ak+1 = 1 · 10k + Ak = 2 k · 5k + Ak. And we know that Ak ≡ 2k
(mod 2 k+1). So Ak+1 ≡ 2k + 2k · 5k ≡ 2k · (1 + 5k) (mod 2 k+1).
1 + 5k is an even number so Ak+1 ≡ 0 (mod 2 k+1).
