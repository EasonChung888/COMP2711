## Question

A height-balanced tree (HBT) is defined recursively as follows.

– A single node $r$ is an HBT with height 0 and size 1.
– Two nodes $u,v$ form an HBT, where $u$ is the root, and $v$ is either $u$'s left or right child. This HBT has height 1 and size 2.
– Let $T_1$ and $T_2$ be two disjoint HBTs with roots $r_1$ and $r_2$, heights $h(T_1)$ and $h(T_2)$, sizes $n(T_1)$ and $n(T_2)$, respectively, such that $|h(T_1)-h(T_2)|\le1$. Create a new node $r$ and make $r_1$ and $r_2$ the left and right child of $r$, respectively. The resulting structure is also an HBT with height $\max(h(T_1),h(T_2))+1$ and size $n(T_1)+n(T_2)+1$.

The figure below shows an HBT $T$ with height $h(T)=4$ and size $n(T)=13$. The numbers are the height differences between the left and right subtrees.

![[knowledge_base/06-Audit/figures/2025S-MID-P05-hbt.png]]

Show that $n(T)\ge f(h(T)+2)-1$ for any HBT $T$, where $f(k)$ is the $k$-th Fibonacci number. Recall that $f(1)=f(2)=1$, and $f(k)=f(k-1)+f(k-2)$ for $k\ge3$.

## Official Answer

Source: [[knowledge_base/past_paper/COMP2711_S2025_Midterm.pdf#page=7|physical PDF page 7]], [[knowledge_base/past_paper/COMP2711_S2025_Midterm.pdf#page=8|physical PDF page 8]].

Base case: For HBT $T$ which is simply a single node $r$, $h(T)=0$ and $n(T)=1$. $f(h(T)+2)-1=f(2)-1=0\le n(T)$.

Inductive step: Assume the proposition holds for some disjoint HBTs $T_1$ and $T_2$ with $|h(T_1)-h(T_2)|\le1$, i.e., $f(h(T_1)+2)-1\le n(T_1)$ and $f(h(T_2)+2)-1\le n(T_2)$.

Consider HBT $T$ rooted at $r$ with left subtree $T_1$ and right subtree $T_2$. By definition, $h(T)=\max(h(T_1),h(T_2))+1$ and $n(T)=n(T_1)+n(T_2)+1$.

Case 1: $h(T_1)=h(T_2)+1$. Then $h(T)=h(T_1)+1=h(T_2)+2$ and
$$\begin{aligned}
f(h(T)+2)-1&=f(h(T)+1)+f(h(T))-1\\
&=f(h(T_1)+2)+f(h(T_2)+2)-1\\
&=(f(h(T_1)+2)-1)+(f(h(T_2)+2)-1)+1\\
&\le n(T_1)+n(T_2)+1=n(T).
\end{aligned}$$
Case 2: $h(T_2)=h(T_1)+1$. The proof is analogous to that for Case 1.

Case 3: $h(T_1)=h(T_2)$. Then $h(T)=h(T_1)+1=h(T_2)+1\ge1$. Note that $f(h(T)+1)=f(h(T))+f(h(T)-1)\ge f(h(T))+0=f(h(T))$, since Fibonacchi numbers are non-negative.
$$\begin{aligned}
f(h(T)+2)-1&=f(h(T)+1)+f(h(T))-1\\
&\le f(h(T)+1)+f(h(T)+1)-1\\
&=(f(h(T_1)+2)-1)+(f(h(T_2)+2)-1)+1\\
&\le n(T_1)+n(T_2)+1=n(T).
\end{aligned}$$
Hence, by structural induction, $n(T)\ge f(h(T)+2)-1$ for any HBT $T$.

## Supplementary Explanation

**Answer.** Every HBT generated by the definition satisfies $n(T)\ge f(h(T)+2)-1$.

**Derivation/proof.** For the single-node constructor, $h=0,n=1$ and $f(2)-1=0\le1$. For the two-node constructor, $h=1,n=2$ and $f(3)-1=1\le2$, whether the child is left or right.

Assume both subtrees satisfy the induction hypothesis and let $H=\max(h_1,h_2)$. If their heights differ, interchange left and right if necessary so that $h_1=H,h_2=H-1$, with $H\ge1$. The merged tree has height $H+1$ and
$$n=n_1+n_2+1\ge f(H+2)+f(H+1)-1=f(H+3)-1.$$
If both heights equal $H\ge0$, then $n\ge2f(H+2)-1$. The initial Fibonacci values and nonnegative recurrence imply $f(H+2)\ge f(H+1)$; at $H=0$ both values are one. Thus $2f(H+2)-1\ge f(H+2)+f(H+1)-1=f(H+3)-1$. Both merge cases satisfy the bound, completing structural induction without using the undefined $f(0)$.
