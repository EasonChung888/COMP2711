## Question

Give a recursive definition of the set of bit strings that have more 0s than 1s.

## Official Answer

Source: [[knowledge_base/past_paper/2021-final-part-1.pdf#page=3|physical PDF page 3]].

> Let $S$ be the set of bit strings that have more 0s than 1s.
>
> **Base case:** $0\in S$.
>
> **Recursive case:** If $x$ and $y$ are bit strings in $S$, then $xy\in S$, $xy1\in S$, $x1y\in S$ and $1xy\in S$.

## Supplementary Explanation

**Answer.** Base: $0\in S$. Rules: from $w=uv\in S$, insert 0, 01, or 10 at the split: $u0v,u01v,u10v\in S$. No other strings belong to S.

**Derivation/proof.** Soundness: the base has zero-minus-one balance 1. Inserting 0 increases this balance by one; inserting 01 or 10 preserves it. Therefore every generated string has more zeros. Completeness: use strong induction on length of a positive-balance string w. If w contains only zeros, construct it by repeated insertion of 0. Otherwise w contains both symbols and hence an adjacent unequal pair 01 or 10 at a transition. Delete that pair to obtain a shorter positive-balance string, which is generated by induction; reinsert the pair at its original split. This generates every required string, including all positions of the minority ones.

The split $w=uv$ may be at either endpoint. After deleting a mixed adjacent pair, the balance remains positive, so the shorter string is nonempty. Reinsertion at the original split recovers the string even when its ones occur internally. The independently derived insertion definition is retained as an alternative to the printed two-string construction, not passed off as its wording.

In the completeness step, deleting that pair preserves the balance, while the length decreases by exactly two. The induction hypothesis supplies the shorter string, and we reinsert it at the original split.
